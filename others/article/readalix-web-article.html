Listening is reading, too

5 min read · Why a voice and a glowing word can change how you get through the web

Most of what we read every day was never designed to be comfortable. Articles stretch on for thousands of words, documentation assumes you will hold three ideas in your head at once, and by late afternoon the letters start to swim. The usual advice is to take breaks. There is a better option: stop making your eyes do all the work.

When a natural voice reads a page aloud while every word lights up in sync, something interesting happens. Your ears carry the text, and your eyes only have to follow the glow. Readers with dyslexia or wandering attention often describe it as the first time a long article felt effortless — the highlight anchors your place on the page, so drifting off for a second costs you nothing. You look back, find the glowing word, and you are exactly where the voice is.

Language learners get a second gift: pronunciation. Seeing a word while hearing it spoken, at a quarter speed if you like, wires the sound to the spelling in a way silent reading never can. And writers discover the oldest editing trick in a new form — your own draft sounds different when someone else reads it back, and the clumsy sentences give themselves away in seconds.

The page reads itself, wherever you are

This is what Readalix does to the web. Select any text, press play, and a natural voice reads it aloud while the sentence glows and the spoken word is highlighted in perfect sync. It works on ordinary articles like this one, and it keeps working where read-aloud tools usually give up: PDFs in Chrome's own viewer, Google Docs, Word Online, and slides in PowerPoint and Google Slides.

The voices are synthesized on your own device — more than a hundred of them, in over thirty languages — so nothing you read ever leaves your browser. Speed the voice up as your ears get faster, pause on the exact word when the phone rings, and resume from that same word when you are back. Reading was never meant to hurt. Give your eyes a break and let the page do the talking.
